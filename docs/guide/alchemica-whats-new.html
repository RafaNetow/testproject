01

A calm, modern portal on any screen

Rebuilt on Material UI with a quiet, minimal look: colour only appears where something needs attention.

What it is

  • Clean, readable pages in plain language, with lab-style figures.
  • Works on phones and tablets; follows dark mode.
  • Each person picks the accent colour they like.
  • A thin bar shows a page is loading; buttons show when they're saving.

Why it matters

  • Clients find their way without training.
  • It looks like a serious, current product — it reflects on Alchemica.
  • Your clients can check progress from the lab floor on their phone.

Where: everywhere. Colour: the palette icon in the top bar.

02

"Waiting on you" and the engagement timeline

The first thing a client sees is what you need from them, and where the engagement stands.

What it is

  • A list of everything waiting on the client — answers, data, reviews, signatures — with due dates and overdue flags.
  • A timeline of the stages; each stage opens to show what happens in it, whether it's on time, and its items.

Why it matters

  • Fewer "where are we?" emails and calls.
  • Clients answer faster when they see exactly what is blocking the work.
  • Delays on their side become visible without you chasing.

Where: client → Executive Summary. You set stages, dates and descriptions under Clients → client → Progress & stages.

03

Chat with Axel, bottom right

A conversation per project, in the round bubble in the corner — where people expect chat on any website.

What it is

  • Live messages both ways, with read receipts and attachments.
  • Opens over any page; full screen on phones.
  • For you: one list of every client conversation, and any message can become a decision or a board card in one click.

Why it matters

  • Agreements stop getting lost in inboxes.
  • Files sent in chat land in the right project automatically.
  • Clients feel close to you without more meetings.

Where: the bubble in the bottom-right corner of every page.

04

Contracts signed inside the portal

Send an NDA, SOW or MSA and the client signs it in the portal — no external account needed.

What it is

  • Upload the PDF and send it for signature.
  • The client reads it, confirms and signs.
  • The signed PDF gets a certificate page: who signed, when, from where, and a fingerprint of the document.

Why it matters

  • Engagements start days sooner.
  • Everything signed sits next to the project, easy to find later.
  • For contracts that need a stronger signature (MitID), Documenso can be connected.

Where: you → project → Contracts. Client → Contracts.

05

Deliverables your clients approve

Reports and plans go to the client for review, and their answer is recorded.

What it is

  • Each deliverable moves from draft to "awaiting client" to approved or changes requested.
  • Drafts stay hidden until you send them.
  • The client's comments are kept with the deliverable.

Why it matters

  • A clear sign-off for every piece of work — useful at invoice time.
  • Change requests come in writing, not by phone.

Where: Deliverables, for you and for the client.

06

Data requests and labelled files

Ask for exactly the data you need, and keep every file labelled by technique.

What it is

  • A checklist of requested data with due dates; the client uploads straight into the request.
  • Files labelled FTIR, DSC, GC-MS, NMR…, with folders and filters.

Why it matters

  • No more "which spreadsheet did you mean?".
  • Data arrives in the right folder, named and dated.
  • Anyone can find the spectra months later.

Where: Files.

07

One calendar for every client

Meetings, due dates, stages and invoices across all your clients, on one calendar.

What it is

  • Month, week and agenda views, with a small date picker to jump around.
  • Filter by kind; click anything to see details or open it.
  • Overdue items are flagged. All times are Copenhagen time.

Why it matters

  • See a busy week coming before it arrives.
  • No deadline hides in a project you haven't opened for a while.

Where: admin → Calendar.

08

Your AI copilot for replies

An assistant that only you see: it drafts replies to clients in your own style and learns from your corrections.

What it is

  • "Suggest reply" in any conversation drafts an answer from the project's facts; you edit it and press Send.
  • It learns your rules ("always end with…") and from how you change its drafts.
  • It writes anonymised case studies from finished projects, for you to review.

Why it matters

  • You answer clients faster, still in your own words.
  • Nothing is ever sent without you, and clients never talk to an AI.
  • Your experience turns into a reusable case book.

Where: chat → "Suggest reply", and admin → Assistant.

09

Nobody misses anything

Every action tells the people involved — in the portal, live, and in a weekly email.

What it is

  • A notification for every relevant action: new deliverable, comment, data received, signature.
  • The bell updates live, without reloading.
  • A Monday email summary for each client contact (they can switch it off).

Why it matters

  • Clients who rarely log in still stay up to date.
  • You see the moment a client approves, uploads or replies.

Where: the bell in the top bar. Email: client → Team → "Email me a weekly summary".

10

Safer and smoother

Under the hood, the portal was reviewed and tested page by page.

Security

  • Each client sees only their own organisation — enforced in the database, not just the screens.
  • A gap that could let a client give themselves admin rights is closed.
  • Only owners see invoices and contracts; collaborators don't.
  • The AI is for you only, with guards against prompt tricks and a log of every draft.

Quality

  • Every page checked as admin, as client and signed out, on desktop and phone.
  • The app's own dropdown menus instead of the browser's.
  • Meeting times always in Copenhagen time, whatever the server.
  • Three demo clients to show the portal without real data.
What comes next

Next step and honest notes

Next: two-way Google Calendar sync

Meetings scheduled in the portal will appear in your Google Calendar and invite the client automatically, with a Google Meet link if you want one. If you move or cancel a meeting in Google, the portal follows. Your own Google events will show on the portal calendar, visible only to you.

It is designed and partly built; it needs a Google Cloud project for Alchemica (about 15 minutes, with you) before it can be switched on.

Worth knowing

  • Signatures. Signing in the portal is a simple electronic signature — fine for NDAs and most SOWs. For contracts that require MitID or an advanced signature, Documenso can be connected.
  • AI provider. The copilot currently uses DeepSeek's API, which processes data outside the EU. Before real client data, it should move to an EU provider with a data processing agreement. Only you use it; clients never see it.
  • Environment. What you see runs in a local test environment. Going live means publishing the database and setting up email, signing and AI with EU-hosted services.
